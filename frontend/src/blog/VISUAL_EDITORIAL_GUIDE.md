# Blog v2 visual editorial guide

Use the visual that helps a reader answer the article's main question. A hero image does not substitute for a useful in-body visual. Consider a contextual figure beside a paragraph when it clarifies a construction detail or breaks up a long explanation; use `layout: left` or `right` for desktop, with a full-width stack on mobile. Use `wide` for a diagram or detail that needs legibility. Do not insert images on a quota or alternate sides mechanically.

For each article, record the reader question, the actual OPC service it connects to, what the reader can decide by the end, the search phrase in the audience's own words, and whether an existing page already answers it. Use a clear title and headings, specific local evidence where applicable, a direct answer, a practical takeaway, and one relevant next step. Review the rendered page on desktop and mobile before requesting approval.

Media may be an OPC project photograph, a licensed contextual photograph, an AI conceptual illustration, or an original diagram. Prefer a relevant OPC photograph when its project, rights, consent, privacy, and claim context are verified. A conceptual AI image must not look like documentary evidence of an OPC job or a real condition at a named address. Check generated depictions for false technical details. A public AI label is an editorial decision based on whether a reader could misunderstand the image; keep its origin in the private provenance record in every case. Do not claim a pictured job is OPC work unless it is verified.

Each production in-body figure needs a marker in the article and reviewed `id`, local `src`, `sha256`, `kind`, `layout`, `alt`, `caption`, `provenance`, `rights`, and `approved: true`. The release gate checks the exact image bytes, including figures in existing posts. The whole post and media require exact-version human publication approval under the Blog v2 policy. A missing useful image is better than a misleading one.

For search, measure qualified queries and clicks in Search Console, page engagement and real inquiries. Do not promise a ranking position or add repeated keywords for their own sake. Google's guidance favors helpful, reliable content with useful images near relevant text and descriptive alt text: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content and https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images .
