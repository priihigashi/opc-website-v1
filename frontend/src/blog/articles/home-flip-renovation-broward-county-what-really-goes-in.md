# A Broward Home Flip: Decide the Renovation Scope Before the Purchase

A flip can look straightforward in photographs: replace finishes, improve the layout, and put the home back on the market. The purchase decision depends on what the photographs cannot show. Before assuming the work is cosmetic, identify the building and permit questions that could change the scope. This is a construction checklist, not a projection of resale price or profit.

## Make the first visit a fact-finding visit

Take a record of the roof, exterior openings, visible cracks or water staining, electrical panel, plumbing fixtures, air-conditioning equipment, and any rooms that appear to have been altered. Photograph the proposed changes and list what remains unknown. A visual visit cannot certify concealed conditions or replace an engineer's or specialist's assessment where one is needed.

Ask for the seller's available plans, survey, and permit history. For an address in the Broward Municipal Services District, the county provides a [permit search by address](https://dpepp.broward.org/BCS/Default.aspx?PossePresentation=ParcelSearchByAddress). For a city property, use that city's building department and records. Compare what was built with what the records show; ask the permitting office how any discrepancy would affect the proposed work.

Check the address in FEMA's [Flood Map Service Center](https://msc.fema.gov/portal/search). If the proposed renovation may alter the building substantially, ask the designer and building department whether flood-related rules or elevation information change the plan. Do this before committing to a layout or construction allowance.

## Separate the work into three scopes

**Required building work:** conditions that must be investigated or corrected to deliver the proposed project safely and obtain the necessary approvals. This may include concealed damage, structural design, utilities, or exterior openings. Do not put an unverified item in this column as if the repair is certain; mark it for investigation.

**Functional improvements:** changes that make the home work better, such as a different kitchen plan, a usable bathroom, or better circulation. Ask whether each change needs another trade or a structural alteration.

**Finish choices:** flooring, cabinetry, tile, paint, lighting, and hardware. These are visible in listing photos, but they are the easiest choices to defer until the first two scopes are understood.

Give every bidder the same three-part list. Request separate assumptions and allowances for work that cannot be confirmed before demolition. A proposal should also say who obtains drawings, applies for permits, schedules inspections, and documents changes.

## Put time in the right order

A practical sequence is investigation, design, permit review where required, demolition and discovery, structural or trade work, required inspections, enclosure, finishes, final inspections, and sale preparation. Some activities overlap, but finish work cannot erase a missing inspection or resolve a design question after the wall is closed.

Ask what can be done before closing on the property, what requires access or seller permission, and which assumptions could delay the start. Do not build a purchase decision on a fixed permit turnaround quoted for another municipality. Broward's [Building Code Division](https://www.broward.org/BUILDING) serves the unincorporated district; cities administer their own building permits.

## Decide whether the project fits your capacity

Before making an offer, write down the largest unresolved construction questions and how you will get answers. If the needed work may include a roof, panel, major plumbing, and a changed floor plan, treat it as a [full renovation](/services/full-renovation), not a cosmetic refresh. If the plan adds floor area, read the [addition scope guide](/how-much-does-a-home-addition-cost-in-broward-county) and test that separate decision.

Verify any contractor you may hire through Florida [DBPR's license search](https://www.myfloridalicense.com/wl11.asp). Request a scope that states what is included, excluded, and still contingent on investigation. If you are weighing a Broward property, bring the address, survey and permit records if available, and the changes you hope to make.
